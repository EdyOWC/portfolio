I once sat down and listed every film I had ever seen. The list came to 1,022.

That number was the raw material for [MVP](/mvp), the experimental short built from one frame per film. But the list turned out to be interesting on its own, before a single frame was cut, because of what it says about when the films I have seen were made.

@widget films-per-decade | 1,022 films by decade of release. Switch between the count, the share and the running total, and select a decade for its numbers.

The shape is steep. The 2000s and 2010s together are more than half of everything — 546 films out of 1,022. The sixty years from 1920 to 1980 come to 209, about a fifth. Two decades of my life outweigh six decades of cinema.

Some of that is simple availability. A film from 2010 is a search away; a film from 1935 needs looking for. Some of it is age: I was alive and watching for the recent ones, and everything older had to be sought out deliberately. And some of it is volume — far more films are made now than were made then, so a random sample of what is watchable skews late by itself.

But the curve still bothers me, because it means my sense of what cinema is was formed almost entirely by the last twenty-five years of it. When I write that a technique is underused, or that a genre has stopped offering new ideas, I am making that claim from a sample that is 53% post-2000. The confidence is not earned evenly across the century.

The 2020s bar is worth a second look. It reads as 165 films in a decade that is not finished, which is already more than the whole of the 1980s and 1990s combined. At that rate the decade will finish somewhere north of 300 — not because I am watching more deliberately, but because watching has become frictionless.

There is a version of this chart I would rather have: films by the decade I *saw* them, not the decade they were made. That one would say something about me rather than about distribution. I did not record it, and it cannot be reconstructed now. If you are keeping a list of what you watch, add the date you watched it. You will want it in ten years.
